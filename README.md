This entry is a single line of the worst advice an AI assistant could
confidently give. The joke is that the advice is delivered in the tidy,
reassuring voice of a helpful assistant while being catastrophically
wrong in a mundane, followable way. The reader can picture themselves
actually doing it and regretting it instantly.

The winning line:

> To fall asleep faster, simply close your eyes and replay every embarrassing thing you've ever said on a loop.

The advice sounds perfectly reasonable on its face — close your eyes,
replay your memories, drift off. But anyone who has ever lain awake at
3 a.m. knows that replaying every embarrassing thing you've ever said
is the exact recipe for staying wide awake, cringing, for hours. The
"on a loop" detail makes it worse: the brain does not just replay the
moment once, it keeps looping it, and the AI cheerfully recommends
that as the path to sleep.

It is concrete (a specific action: replay embarrassing memories on a
loop), it is followable (you really could try it), it lands on the
first read, and it sounds exactly like an AI assistant's tidy,
confident voice. No shock value, no danger — just confidently wrong,
mundane advice.

## How it works

`main.py` is a minimal, deterministic Python 3 program using only the
standard library. It holds the chosen line as a string literal, runs a
self-check against the competition gate (1-140 Unicode code points, not
only whitespace, no newlines), and then writes the line — with no
trailing newline — to `/output/entry.txt`. Running it twice produces
byte-identical output.
